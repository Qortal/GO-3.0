# Practical Life Skills

*Everyday competence — the quiet foundation under all preparedness.*

## The home tool kit that handles 90% of jobs

Claw hammer, multi-bit screwdriver (PH/PZ/flat/Torx), adjustable wrench,
locking pliers and needle-nose pliers, utility knife with spare blades,
tape measure, spirit level, handsaw, cordless drill with bit set, hex
keys, duct tape and electrical tape, super glue and two-part epoxy, wood
glue, WD-40-type penetrating oil and a proper lubricating oil (penetrant
frees things; oil keeps them free — they are not the same), assorted
screws/nails/wall plugs, zip ties, wire, sandpaper, work gloves, safety
glasses, headlamp. Add a stud/cable finder before drilling walls and a
voltage tester pen before touching anything electrical.

## Know your shutoffs (the 10-minute audit that saves houses)

Tonight, locate and physically test: the main water shutoff (and label
it), water heater valve, gas shutoff (know which tool it needs and keep it
there), and the main electrical breaker. Everyone in the household should
know all four. Burst pipe response = water off, then electricity off if
water nears wiring, then drain taps. Gas smell = no switches or flames,
everyone out, valve off if safely reachable, call the emergency line from
outside.

## Repairs worth learning (each ~30 minutes to learn)

Unclog drains (plunger technique, trap removal — a bucket underneath, and
skip the caustic chemicals which make later work dangerous); stop a
running toilet (usually flapper or float adjustment); replace a tap
washer/cartridge; patch drywall holes; re-hang a door that sticks (tighten
hinge screws first — try a longer screw into the frame); bleed radiators;
replace plugs and fuses where legal; bicycle basics (puncture repair,
brake adjustment, chain care — a working bicycle is grid-down transport);
and car basics (change a wheel, jump-start correctly, check oil/coolant,
replace wipers and bulbs).

## Sharpening and tool care

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one — it cuts where aimed with less
force. Learn one system well: a two-sided whetstone (coarse ~400-1000 grit
to set an edge, fine ~3000+ to polish) at a consistent ~20° angle, edge
trailing, until a burr forms and is stripped; finish on a strop or
cardboard. Scissors, axes, chisels, and garden tools all sharpen with the
same logic (file for axes and spades). Tools wiped clean and lightly oiled
don't rust; wooden handles get linseed oil yearly.

## Mending: clothes and gear

A sewing kit (needles, strong polyester thread, patches, safety pins,
buttons, scissors) plus four skills covers most textile failures: sewing a
button, whip-stitching a seam, patching (iron-on backed by stitches for
anything load-bearing), and darning socks over a jar lid. Add fabric glue,
seam sealer, and repair tape for waterproof gear; shoe repair
(contact-cement a delaminating sole, replace laces with paracord) extends
boots for years. Wool clothing mends invisibly and lasts decades — buy
quality once, repair forever.

## Household chemistry that replaces a shelf of products

White vinegar (descaling, glass, fabric softener, weed spray), baking
soda (scouring, odors, drain refresh with vinegar), citric acid (kettles,
bathroom scale), soap flakes or Castile soap (nearly everything), washing
soda (laundry booster, degreasing), and isopropyl alcohol (disinfecting,
sticker residue). Never mix bleach with vinegar, other acids, or ammonia —
the fumes are dangerous. These staples are cheap, store forever, and
matter double when supply chains hiccup.

## Kitchen resilience skills

Baking bread from flour, water, salt, and time (a no-knead loaf or
sourdough — a starter is flour + water + patience and never needs bought
yeast again); stretching meals from staples (soups, stews, beans-and-rice
patterns from the storage pantry); using every part (stock from bones and
peels, fat saved for cooking); cast-iron care (season with thin oil coats,
never soak); and cooking every recipe you store *before* you need it.

## Paperwork, money, and digital resilience

One binder: ID copies, insurance policies, property documents, medical
summaries and prescriptions, key contacts — with a waterproof copy in the
go-bag and an encrypted digital copy (offline USB in the Faraday bin, plus
one off-site). Financial buffer in layers: cash at home (small notes),
an emergency fund, and minimal recurring costs — frugality is resilience.
Password manager with printed recovery codes stored with documents; backups
of irreplaceable data following 3-2-1 (three copies, two media, one
off-site). Self-hosted and decentralized services (your own node, local
files) keep working when platforms don't.

## Learning and community as infrastructure

The meta-skill: fix one new thing per month with a manual or video before
calling a professional — competence compounds. Keep paper references (this
handbook, a repair manual, a first-aid guide, a regional plant guide).
Know your neighbors' skills and share yours; borrow and lend tools; join
or start the local networks (repair cafés, garden groups, radio clubs,
seed swaps) — in every real emergency, the people nearby are the actual
emergency service.
