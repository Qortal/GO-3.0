## Q: How much sleep do adults need?

A: Most adults do best with about 7–9 hours of sleep per night. Consistency of sleep and wake times often matters as much as total hours. Cool, dark, quiet rooms and an earlier caffeine cutoff help many people. This is general education, not a personal medical target.

## Q: What is a good bedroom temperature for sleep?

A: Many people sleep better in a cool room, roughly 16–19 °C, that is dark and quiet. Overheating can fragment sleep. Adjust for comfort and safety, especially for infants and older adults.

Also asked as: cool room for sleep · sleep temperature Celsius

## Q: When should I stop caffeine before bed?

A: As a practical rule of thumb, finish caffeine at least 8 hours before bedtime, because it can linger and reduce sleep quality. Sensitivity varies. If sleep is poor, try an earlier cutoff for a week and notice the difference.

Also asked as: caffeine cutoff · coffee before sleep

## Q: How does morning light help health?

A: Bright natural light soon after waking helps set the body's circadian clock, which supports alertness by day and sleep readiness at night. Aim for outdoor light within about an hour of waking when possible. Do not stare at the sun.

Also asked as: morning sunlight · get light after waking

## Q: How can I reduce evening screen light disruption?

A: Dim lights and reduce bright screens in the 2–3 hours before bed to support melatonin timing. Blue-blocking glasses or night modes can help when screens are unavoidable. Consistent sleep timing still matters more than any gadget.

Also asked as: blue light at night · screens before bed

## Q: What is the basic movement prescription for health?

A: A strong base is frequent low-intensity movement (walking, chores, standing) plus 2–3 strength sessions per week and occasional higher-intensity work. Muscle supports glucose use, injury resilience, and healthy aging. Progress gradually if you are new to training.

Also asked as: daily movement basics · how much should I move

## Q: Why is walking after meals useful?

A: A short walk after meals can blunt blood-glucose spikes for many people and aids digestion comfort. Even 10–15 minutes helps. It is a lifestyle habit, not a treatment plan for diabetes — medicated individuals should follow their clinician's advice.

Also asked as: post meal walk · walk after eating

## Q: How often should I do strength training?

A: For general health, 2–3 full-body strength sessions per week covering squat, hinge, push, pull, and carry patterns is a widely used template. Rest days matter for recovery. Pain that alters form, or known injuries, need professional guidance.

Also asked as: lift weights frequency · resistance training schedule

## Q: How does chronic stress affect the body?

A: Ongoing stress can elevate cortisol and undermine sleep, digestion, immunity, and appetite regulation. Practical supports include sleep priority, outdoor time, social connection, and slow breathing. This is educational — anxiety or trauma symptoms deserve professional support.

Also asked as: stress cortisol health · chronic stress effects

## Q: What breathwork helps calm stress?

A: Slow nasal breathing around 5–6 breaths per minute, or a longer exhale (for example 4 in, 8 out), often downshifts arousal. Box breathing (equal inhale–hold–exhale–hold) is another common tool. Intense hyperventilation practices must never be done in or near water or while driving.

Also asked as: slow breathing for calm · extended exhale breathing

## Q: Is sunlight good for vitamin D and mood?

A: Sensible sun exposure supports vitamin D production and many people report better mood with daylight outdoors. Avoid burning; cover or shade when UV is intense. People with skin cancer risk or photosensitizing medications should follow clinician guidance.

Also asked as: sun exposure vitamin D · sensible sun

## Q: What are basic cold exposure ideas for beginners?

A: Proponents often start with ending a shower in 30–90 seconds of cold water and progress slowly. Reported aims include alertness and stress practice, not proven cure-alls. Never immerse alone, never combine breath-holds with water, and exit if dizzy or numb.

Also asked as: cold shower start · deliberate cold basics

## Q: What are basic sauna safety themes?

A: Community patterns often cite 3–4 sessions weekly of about 15–25 minutes at roughly 80–100 °C for traditional saunas, with deliberate rehydration. Exit at dizziness; avoid alcohol around sessions. Cardiovascular disease or pregnancy needs medical guidance before heat practices.

Also asked as: sauna how often · heat exposure basics

## Q: What does 'eat real food' mean practically?

A: A high-leverage change is replacing ultra-processed products with whole foods: meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, properly prepared dairy, and traditional fats like olive oil, butter, or tallow. Reading labels and choosing single-ingredient foods removes much engineered overconsumption.

Also asked as: whole foods vs ultra processed · nutrient dense eating

## Q: Why avoid ultra-processed foods?

A: Ultra-processed foods often combine refined starch, sugar, industrial seed oils, and additives designed for overeating. They tend to displace protein, fiber, and micronutrients. Occasional treats differ from a daily base diet built on them.

Also asked as: ultra processed food problem · seed oils sugar starch

## Q: How does poor sleep affect hunger and blood sugar?

A: Short or fragmented sleep can worsen blood-sugar control and disrupt hunger hormones, increasing cravings and next-day fatigue. Protecting sleep is often more impactful than adding another supplement. Persistent insomnia needs clinical assessment.

Also asked as: sleep and cravings · sleep blood sugar

## Q: Is alcohol good for sleep?

A: Alcohol may help some people fall asleep faster but typically fragments sleep and reduces restorative stages. Performance-oriented sleep guides treat alcohol as a sleep disruptor, not an aid. Dependence or cutting back difficulty warrants professional help.

Also asked as: alcohol sleep quality · nightcap myth

## Q: How does muscle help blood sugar?

A: Skeletal muscle is a major site of glucose uptake; building and using muscle often improves how the body handles carbohydrate. Strength training plus daily walking is a practical combo. This is education, not diabetes treatment advice.

Also asked as: muscle glucose disposal · strength training insulin sensitivity

## Q: How can I start strength training safely at home?

A: Begin with bodyweight squat-to-chair, hip hinges with light load, push-ups on an incline, rows with a band, and loaded carries you can walk with tall posture. Focus on controlled form 2–3 days weekly. Sharp joint pain means stop and get assessed.

Also asked as: beginner home strength · bodyweight strength start

## Q: Why prioritize foundations before biohacks?

A: Sleep, whole food, daily movement, daylight, and stress skills usually outperform gadgets and advanced protocols. Layer technology only after those are consistent. Contested biohacks should be treated as experiments, not settled medicine.

Also asked as: basics before supplements · optimize sleep first

## Q: What temperature range is often recommended for sleep?

A: A cool (about 16–19 °C), dark, quiet room is a common sleep-hygiene target for adults. Individual comfort varies; older adults may prefer slightly warmer. Infants have separate safe-sleep rules — follow pediatric guidance.

Also asked as: 16 to 19 C sleep · bedroom cool dark quiet

## Q: How much water do adults typically need?

A: A common educational estimate is roughly 30–35 ml of fluid per kilogram of body weight per day, more in heat or heavy exertion. Thirst, pale-yellow urine, and stable energy are practical checks. Exact needs vary; this is not a medical prescription.

Also asked as: daily fluid needs · ml per kg water

## Q: What are early signs of dehydration?

A: Common early signs include dark urine, dry mouth, headache, and fatigue. Heat, illness with vomiting or diarrhea, and endurance work raise risk. Confusion, fainting, or inability to keep fluids down needs urgent care. For ordinary fluid loss from diarrhea/vomiting, sip oral rehydration (1 L safe water + 6 tsp sugar + ½ tsp salt).

Also asked as: dehydration symptoms · dark urine headache · am I dehydrated · signs of dehydration

## Q: Do I need electrolytes every day?

A: Many people meet electrolyte needs through food and ordinary salt use. Needs rise with heavy sweating, hot climates, low-carb diets, or prolonged fasting. Seek care for severe weakness, irregular heartbeat, or confusion rather than self-treating aggressively.

Also asked as: daily electrolytes · salt potassium magnesium

## Q: Why do low-carb diets increase electrolyte needs?

A: When carbohydrate and insulin drop, kidneys excrete more sodium and water, which can deplete related electrolytes. Communities teaching 'healthy keto' emphasize sodium, potassium, magnesium, and adequate water. Persistent symptoms deserve clinical review.

Also asked as: keto electrolytes · keto flu salt

## Q: What is keto flu?

A: Keto flu refers to transient fatigue, headache, irritability, or cramps some people feel when first cutting carbs sharply. Extra fluids and electrolytes often help; it is not a formal medical diagnosis. Chest pain, severe weakness, or fainting is not 'flu' — get care.

Also asked as: keto flu symptoms · low carb adaptation flu

## Q: What is oral rehydration for diarrhea?

A: For ordinary adult gastroenteritis education: 1 litre of safe water with 6 level teaspoons sugar and ½ level teaspoon salt, sipped frequently, can replace losses. Children dehydrate faster and need prompt clinical advice. Bloody stool, high fever, or inability to drink is urgent.

Also asked as: ORS homemade · WHO oral rehydration

## Q: What electrolytes matter most when sweating heavily?

A: Sodium is usually the dominant sweat electrolyte loss; potassium and magnesium also matter over long efforts. Practical response is water plus salty food or an electrolyte product used as labeled. Collapse, confusion, or seizure risk situations need emergency care.

Also asked as: sweat electrolytes · sodium loss exercise

## Q: How much extra fluid in hot weather?

A: Heat and humidity raise fluid losses through sweat; increase intake and include sodium-containing foods when sweating heavily. Rest in shade and cool the body if overheating. Heat stroke signs (confusion, very hot skin, collapse) are emergencies.

Also asked as: heat hydration increase · summer water needs

## Q: Can low salt cause problems on keto?

A: Educational keto guidance often increases salt intake because insulin-driven sodium retention falls. Headache and lightheadedness early on are commonly linked to this shift. People with blood-pressure disease or on related drugs must involve their clinician before changing salt habits.

Also asked as: salt on low carb · sodium keto

## Q: What fluids help with fever at home?

A: During ordinary fevers, frequent sips of water, oral rehydration solution, or broth help replace losses. Seek urgent care for stiff neck, rash with fever, breathing trouble, confusion, seizures, or a fever in young infants. This is not a dosing guide for medicines.

Also asked as: fever hydration · drink when feverish

## Q: What is the insulin model of fat storage taught by Dr. Berg-style keto education?

A: In themes popularized by Dr. Eric Berg DC, chronically high insulin from frequent eating and refined carbs is described as locking the body in fat-storage mode. Lowering insulin via fewer carbs and longer gaps between meals is said to allow fat and ketone use. This is an educational model, not settled universal fact, and not personal medical advice.

Also asked as: Berg insulin model · insulin locks fat storage

## Q: What is 'healthy keto' as taught in Berg-style materials?

A: In Berg-style educational themes, healthy keto is nutrient-dense low-carb eating: large volumes of non-starchy vegetables, adequate protein, healthy fats to satiety, and avoidance of ultra-processed 'keto junk'. Typical teaching cites roughly 20–50 g net carbs daily. Medical conditions and medications require professional supervision.

Also asked as: healthy keto meaning · nutrient dense low carb

## Q: What does a healthy keto plate look like?

A: A common teaching pattern: 2–4 large handfuls of non-starchy vegetables, a palm to two of quality protein, and fat to satiety from foods like olive oil, butter/ghee, avocado, or fatty cuts. Avoid sugar, flour, sweet drinks, and industrial seed oils. Individual needs differ; this is education only.

Also asked as: keto plate pattern · Berg what to eat

## Q: How do you start intermittent fasting gradually?

A: Berg-style fasting education often progresses: cut snacks → three meals with nothing between → 16:8 → 18:6 → OMAD only if comfortable. Hunger may come in waves that pass. Pregnant people, children, underweight individuals, type 1 diabetics, and those on glucose- or blood-pressure drugs need professional guidance and often should not fast.

## Q: Who should not try fasting without medical guidance?

A: Educational consensus even within fasting communities: seek professional guidance and often avoid unsupervised fasting if you have type 1 diabetes, use glucose-lowering or blood-pressure medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are a child, are underweight, or have an eating-disorder history. Long fasts are advanced practices.

Also asked as: fasting contraindications · who should not fast

## Q: How long is a beginner fasting window?

A: A common beginner pattern is 16:8 — about 16 hours fasting and an 8-hour eating window — after snacks are already eliminated. Start from your current schedule rather than jumping to long fasts. Dizziness, fainting, or medication issues means stop and get advice.

## Q: How should you break a longer fast gently?

A: Educational guidance usually starts with small gentle foods — broth, eggs, cooked vegetables — rather than a huge feast, to reduce rebound overeating and digestive shock. Fasts beyond about 24–48 hours are advanced and commonly advised only with medical oversight.

## Q: Can fasting help insulin resistance according to Berg-style teaching?

## Q: Is keto a good idea during pregnancy?

## Q: Why might medicated diabetics be at risk with keto or fasting?

A: Ketogenic diets and fasting can substantially change glucose and insulin needs. People on glucose-lowering medication can develop hypoglycemia if doses are not supervised and adjusted by their prescriber. Never change diabetes drugs on your own.

## Q: Should children do intermittent fasting?

## Q: Are long fasts beyond 24 hours a good starting point?

## Q: What does 'eat until satisfied in the window' mean?

## Q: How is HRV used in recovery tracking?

## Q: What stroke signs need immediate help?

## Q: When is a fever concerning enough for urgent care?

## Q: What wound infection signs mean I should see a clinician?

## Q: When is shortness of breath an emergency?

Mild shortness of breath after hard exercise that settles quickly is different. Known asthma plans still follow your clinician's written instructions.

## Q: When should abdominal pain get urgent care?

## Q: What head injury signs need emergency assessment?

## Q: When is dehydration in children especially dangerous?

## Q: What allergic reaction signs are emergencies?

A: Difficulty breathing, swelling of tongue/throat, widespread hives with collapse, or feeling of impending doom after an allergen exposure is an emergency — use prescribed epinephrine if available and call emergency services. Mild itchy skin differs from anaphylaxis.

## Q: When should I seek care for a tick bite?

A: Remove ticks promptly with fine tweezers at the skin, steady pull, then disinfect and note the date. Expanding rash or flu-like illness in following weeks warrants medical attention. Do not burn or twist ticks out.

Also asked as: tick bite doctor · Lyme rash watch

## Q: What bleeding situations need emergency help?

A: Bleeding that soaks dressings quickly, spurts, follows major trauma, or accompanies confusion/weakness needs emergency care. Apply firm direct pressure continuously. Tourniquets are for life-threatening limb bleeding when pressure fails — training matters.

Also asked as: severe bleeding emergency · won't stop bleeding

## Q: When are burns emergencies?

A: Large, deep, facial, genital, airway, or circumferential burns, or burns with electrical/chemical injury, need emergency care. Cool ordinary minor burns under cool running water for about 20 minutes, then cover loosely. Do not apply ice or butter.

Also asked as: serious burn care · burn emergency

## Q: When should pregnancy-related symptoms get immediate care?

Also asked as: pregnancy warning signs · pregnant bleeding pain

## Q: What are early signs of heat exhaustion?

## Q: What is heat stroke and why is it an emergency?

A: Heat stroke is a medical emergency with possible confusion, fainting, very high body temperature, and hot skin that may be dry or wet. Call emergency services and begin aggressive cooling while waiting. Do not leave the person alone.

## Q: How can I prevent heat illness during work or sport?

## Q: What are early hypothermia signs?

## Q: How should you warm someone with mild cold stress?

## Q: Why is combining breath-holds with cold water dangerous?

## Q: Can fans or shade help in heat waves without air conditioning?

## Q: What is basic daily oral care without a dentist visit?

A: Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste if available, clean between teeth once daily, and limit frequent sugar/acid sips. This maintains gums and enamel between professional visits. Swelling, fever, or spreading facial infection needs urgent care.

## Q: How can I manage a toothache until I reach care?

A: Rinse with warm salt water, avoid very hot/cold triggers, use temporary dental filling material if a cavity is open, and consider clove oil on a cotton pellet only around the tooth as traditional temporary comfort — keep it off soft tissue. Facial swelling or fever needs urgent professional care; this is not a cure.

## Q: What belongs in a dental emergency kit?

## Q: How should I handle a knocked-out adult tooth?

Keeping it in milk or saliva are commonly taught temporary storage options when instructions are known. Baby teeth are handled differently — get professional advice.

## Q: Why do sugary drinks harm teeth?
