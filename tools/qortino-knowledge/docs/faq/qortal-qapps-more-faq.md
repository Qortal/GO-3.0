## Q: What is Q-Drive?

A: Q-Drive is a private drive Q-App on Qortal, published under the name Q-Drive. Your files, their names and your folders are encrypted on your device before anything is published to QDN, so only your account, and people you share with, can open them. It has folders, search, stars, a trash, previews for most file types, sharing, and a Notes tab for notes and to-do lists.

## Q: How do I open Q-Drive?

A: Q-Drive runs inside Qortal Hub on a computer or Qortal GO on a phone. Open it from the apps list there, or with the link qortal://APP/Q-Drive. In a normal web browser it only shows a note asking you to open it in Hub or GO, because it needs your Qortal account to encrypt and publish.

## Q: What do I need before I can use Q-Drive?

A: You need a Qortal account signed in to Hub or GO, a registered name, and a little QORT for fees. Q-Drive keeps your encrypted files under your Qortal name, so with no name it asks you to register one in Hub (1.25 QORT), wait for it to confirm, then come back. The Notes tab works without a name, but your notes then stay on that device only.

## Q: How do I create my drive in Q-Drive?

A: 1. Open Q-Drive in Hub or GO and let it ask Hub which account to use.
2. On "Create your private drive", choose whether to set a vault passphrase now (optional extra protection).
3. Tap "Create my drive (0.01 QORT)" and confirm in Hub.
If you used Q-Drive with this name before, tap Look again first: your node may still be catching up, and Q-Drive won't make a second drive while it can't tell.

## Q: How much does Q-Drive cost to use?

A: There is no subscription. Every publish costs 0.01 QORT per resource and always waits for your tap: creating the drive is 0.01, each file is 0.01 plus 0.01 to update your file list (two files cost 0.03), saving other changes is 0.01, and a share is 0.02 (0.03 with a Q-Mail notice). Opening and downloading your own files costs nothing. Hub shows the cost and asks you to confirm each time.

## Q: How do I add files to Q-Drive?

A: Use New, then Publish files (or Publish folder), or the + button on a phone. On a computer you can also drag files onto the drive ("Drop to publish"). Q-Drive shows the cost, encrypts each file on your device, then publishes it to QDN after you confirm in Hub. Q-Drive says "publish" rather than "upload" because the files go to your node and the peer network, not to a company server.

## Q: How do I share a file with one person in Q-Drive?

A: 1. Select one or more files and choose Share with a person.
2. Type their registered name and add a note if you like.
3. Confirm in Hub. It costs 0.02 QORT (0.01 for the share and 0.01 for your file list), or 0.03 if you also send a Q-Mail.
The share shows in their Shared with me, and only they can open it. They get the keys to those files only, never the rest of your drive.

From a share you can open or Download a file, or tap Save to my drive to keep an encrypted copy in your own drive. You don't need a drive of your own just to see what was shared with you.

## Q: Who can see my files in Q-Drive?

A: Anyone on QDN can see that your name uses Q-Drive, how many encrypted files and shares it stores, how big each file is and when they were published. They can't see file contents, names, types, folders or notes. People you share with see only the files you gave them, and Q-Share copies are public for good. Settings, Security, "Who can see what" lists it all.

## Q: What is the Notes tab in Q-Drive?

A: Notes (in Q-Drive 1.1 and later) is a tab for plain-text notes and to-do lists. They are encrypted on your device and saved to QDN under your name when you tap Save (0.01 QORT). You can colour, pin, archive and search notes, add due dates and timers, and set an optional Notes passphrase. Notes works even if you never create a drive; open it from the Notes tab or with qortal://APP/Q-Drive/notes.

## Q: What is the vault passphrase in Q-Drive?

A: It is optional extra protection for your drive key, set in Settings under Security (or when you create the drive). Without it, any other app running in Hub could ask Hub to unlock your drive key without asking you; with it, they can't unless they guess the passphrase. You type it each time you unlock. Set it early: one added later can't take back a key an app already read.

Also asked as: q-drive vault passphrase · q-drive password · q-drive passphrase · lock q-drive with passphrase · q-drive extra protection

## Q: What happens if I forget my Q-Drive vault passphrase?

A: Then nobody can open your drive: not you, not Qortal, not anyone. There is no reset. Q-Drive asks you to tick "I understand that a lost passphrase means a lost drive" before it sets one, so write it down somewhere safe and offline. A hint is optional, but other apps in Hub could read it, so never put the passphrase itself in the hint.

Also asked as: forgot q-drive passphrase · lost q-drive vault passphrase · reset q-drive passphrase · q-drive passphrase recovery

## Q: What does Lock now do in Q-Drive?

A: Lock now makes Q-Drive forget your drive key, your file list and every decrypted file from memory, and it locks Notes too. Q-Drive also locks itself when idle: Settings, Security, Auto-lock offers after 15 minutes, 1 hour (the default), 4 hours or never. Locking protects you from a harmful app opened later; with a vault passphrase you type it again to unlock.

Also asked as: q-drive lock now · q-drive auto-lock · lock my q-drive · q-drive locks itself · q-drive locked after being idle

## Q: Can other Q-Apps read my Q-Drive files?

A: Without a vault passphrase, an app could ask Hub to unlock your drive key without asking you. With one, it can still read your passphrase hint, shares sent to you and your Q-Mails, and could try to guess a weak passphrase. While your drive is unlocked, a harmful app open at the same time might reach Q-Drive's page, so lock Q-Drive before you open apps you don't trust.

Also asked as: q-drive other apps · q-drive malicious app · can apps read q-drive · q-drive security other q-apps · q-drive hub apps decrypt

## Q: Does deleting a file in Q-Drive remove it from QDN?

A: No. Move to Trash keeps the item restorable until you Delete forever or Empty trash, then Save changes. Deleting only removes it from your drive: the encrypted data stays on QDN, anyone you shared it with still has its key, and copies on Q-Share or sent with Q-Mail stay where they are. Nobody else can read the encrypted data without a key.

## Q: Which files can Q-Drive preview?

A: Q-Drive previews images, video, audio, PDFs, text, code, Markdown, spreadsheets, Word files and ZIP archives inside the app. Web pages are shown without scripts, forms or outside pictures. Very large files may show only their start; download them to open with another app. A damaged or crafted file from someone else could make the page stop responding, so Q-Drive warns before previewing shared files.

## Q: How big can a file be in Q-Drive?

A: Hub publishes files up to 2000 MB each. On a public node the limit is 500 MB per file, and some older Hub versions take only 200 MB from apps; Q-Drive tells you which limit applies. Q-Mail attachments are limited to 40 MB, so for bigger files send a Secure Q-Drive link instead.

## Q: Why does Q-Drive warn me that I'm on a public node?

A: A public node keeps what you publish only for a while. Unless a node of your own reads your files and your file list soon, they can disappear for good. To keep them, use Q-Drive in Hub with your own node, or have your own node follow your name so it fetches and keeps what you publish. "Got it" hides the warning until Q-Drive starts again.

Due dates sync with your notes and show as chips such as "Due today" or "Overdue", but Q-Drive doesn't send reminders for them.

## Q: Where do Q-Weather forecasts come from?

## Q: How do I read the Q-Weather chart?

A: Days run left to right, with each day's high (orange) and low (blue) above. Orange shading means warmer than the day before, blue colder, and a violet line marks freezing. The thin red line is now: drag across the chart to move the thick red line, and the map follows.
