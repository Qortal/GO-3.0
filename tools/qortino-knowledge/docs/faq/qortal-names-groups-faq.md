## Q: What can I do with a registered name on Qortal?

A: A name is the readable identity of your account. People can send you QORT, a direct message or a Q-Mail using your name instead of your long address, and tag you with @ in chat. A name is also needed to publish on QDN (apps, websites, videos, files) and to set an avatar. What you publish is tied to the name, so it can later be sold together with that data.

## Q: What can't I do on Qortal without a registered name?

A: Without a name you can still hold and send QORT and take part in groups and chats; others then see your address. A name is needed to publish anything on QDN (apps, websites, videos, files), to set an avatar, to rate Q-Apps and to save your pinned Q-Apps to QDN. Most publishing Q-Apps, such as Torq, Q-Tube and Q-Mail, expect one too.

## Q: What is a primary name?

A: It is the main public identity of your account. Hub and GO show it in chats, member lists and payments, and Hub publishes under it, for example your avatar. With one name, that name is your primary. With several, the primary is the earliest one you got and still own, and the others are secondary names.

## Q: What characters can I use in a Qortal name?

A: Letters from any language, numbers and spaces are allowed. A name can't start or end with a space, can't contain double spaces, and can't be a Qortal address. The capitals you type are kept, but for uniqueness the network ignores case, accents and look-alike letters.

## Q: How long can a Qortal name be?

A: Between 3 and 40 characters. The limit is counted in bytes, so letters outside the basic Latin alphabet take more room and fewer of them fit. Group names follow a different limit of 3 to 32.

## Q: Are Qortal names case sensitive?

## Q: Can two accounts have the same Qortal name?

A: No. Each name exists once on the blockchain and belongs to one account at a time. Because names that differ only by capitals, accents or look-alike letters count as the same, nobody can register a near copy of yours, such as swapping a lowercase l for a capital I. A name with an extra letter or number is a different name, though, so check the address when it matters.

## Q: What is the difference between a group and a channel?

A: A group is the community: an on-chain list of members with an owner and admins. Channels are the conversation rooms inside a group, one per topic, created by its admins and sorted into categories. Joining the group gives you its channels, except admin channels, which only admins see.

## Q: How is Group Score calculated?

A: It adds four parts: QORT holdings of the members count for 50%, activity for 30% (people posting in the last 7 days, messages in 7 days and in 24 hours), community size for 10%, and legacy for 10%, one point per full year since the group was created, up to 10. Holdings use a log scale, so one very large holder can't flatten the differences between groups. The result is rounded to 0 to 100.

## Q: What are the advanced options when creating a group?

A: It is the share of the group's admins that must approve a decision sent as a group transaction before it takes effect. The choices are None, One, or 20 to 100% of admins, and Hub's default is 40%. In an ordinary group, a transaction an admin sends through the group is approved automatically, while one from a regular member waits for the admins' approvals.

A: Re-encrypting normally shares the group's existing keys with the current member list. Add a new key also adds a fresh key, which only the current members receive. Hub asks you to do that sparingly, because every added key makes the group's key file bigger.

Also asked as: add a new group key · new group key option · add new key sparingly · rotate group key

## Q: Can people removed from a private group still read its encrypted content?

A: They keep any keys they already received, so content encrypted with those keys may stay readable to them. Re-encrypting sends the keys only to the current members, and Add a new key creates a key that only current members get, which protects what is encrypted from then on.

Also asked as: kicked member read old content · removed member group key · banned member encrypted data · lock out removed member

## Q: Why does Hub ask the group owner to re-encrypt the key?

A: When the newest group key was published by an admin who isn't the owner, Hub shows the owner 'please re-encrypt the key as a safeguard'. Re-encrypting it yourself makes sure the key members use comes from you. It is a normal key publish with a small fee.

## Q: Do group admins need a registered name to publish group keys?

A: Yes. Group keys are published on QDN under the admin's registered name, like any other publish. That is also why selling or renaming that name affects the keys: another admin should re-encrypt them first.

## Q: Can a Q-App encrypt data that only a group's admins can read?

## Q: Why does Hub say No name registered?

## Q: Can a registered name be deleted?

## Q: What do REGISTER_NAME, UPDATE_NAME, SELL_NAME and BUY_NAME mean in an approval?

A: They are the blockchain transaction types for names. REGISTER_NAME registers a new name, UPDATE_NAME renames it, SELL_NAME lists it for sale, CANCEL_SELL_NAME takes it off the market, and BUY_NAME buys a listed name at its price. The approval window names the type and shows the fee before you accept.

Also asked as: register_name transaction · update_name meaning · sell_name buy_name · cancel_sell_name meaning · name transaction types

## Q: What do JOIN_GROUP, GROUP_INVITE and GROUP_KICK mean in an approval?

A: They are the blockchain transaction types for groups. CREATE_GROUP makes a group, JOIN_GROUP joins one (or sends a join request), LEAVE_GROUP leaves, GROUP_INVITE invites someone or accepts their request, CANCEL_GROUP_INVITE withdraws an invite, GROUP_KICK and GROUP_BAN remove a member, CANCEL_GROUP_BAN lifts a ban, ADD_GROUP_ADMIN and REMOVE_GROUP_ADMIN change admins, and UPDATE_GROUP changes the group's settings or owner.

Also asked as: join_group transaction · group_invite meaning · group_ban leave_group · add_group_admin meaning · group transaction types

## Q: Can two groups have the same name?

A: No. Group names are unique, and like registered names they ignore capitals and look-alike letters, so a new group can't reuse an existing group's name with that kind of small change. A group name is 3 to 32 characters long.

## Q: Do I need a registered name to create a group?

A: Not to create it: the blockchain only asks for the small fee. For a closed group you will want one, though, because an admin publishes the group's encryption key under their registered name, and members can't read its private content until a key exists.

## Q: How do I make an announcements channel in my group?

## Q: Can I invite many people to my group at once?

A: Invite member sends one invite at a time, each with its own small fee. To reach many people, share the group's invite link instead: for an open group they can join straight away, and for a closed group the link sends a request an admin can accept.

## Q: What does Pending mean on a group in Find groups?

## Q: What does it mean when a group has no owner?

A: Some important groups, such as the MINTER group, were handed to the null account, an address nobody controls. In such a group no single person can act alone: invites, kicks, bans and admin changes only take effect once enough of its admins approve them with group approval transactions.

Also asked as: group owned by null account · group without an owner · null owner group · no one owns the group

## Q: Can someone be banned from a group before they join?

A: Yes. The owner can ban any account, member or not. A ban on someone who isn't a member yet removes any invite or join request they have and stops them joining until it is lifted.

## Q: Can I undo a kick or a ban?

A: A ban can be lifted: on the group's Members screen, open Bans and press Cancel ban. A kick can't be undone as such, but the person can come back: they can rejoin an open group, and for a closed group an admin can invite them again. Each of these is a small on-chain transaction.

## Q: How can my group get a higher Group Score?
