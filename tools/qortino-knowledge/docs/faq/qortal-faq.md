## Q: What is Qortal?

A: Qortal is a fully decentralized blockchain and peer-to-peer data network — infrastructure for a parallel internet. People run their own nodes, mint QORT by contributing (minting), and use QDN to publish websites, Q-Apps, chat, and media without corporate cloud ownership. No ICO and no company owns the network. ## Q: What is minting on Qortal?

A: On Qortal, minting replaces mining. An account with an active minting key that stays online helps forge blocks and earns QORT. Reward share grows with your level (time spent minting). You cannot buy faster minting with expensive hardware — ordinary hardware (even a Raspberry Pi class machine) is enough. One minting account per person is the rule.

## Q: How do I become a minter?

A: ## How to become a minter

1. **Register a Qortal name** and keep a little QORT for fees.
2. **Run a synced full node** (Qortal Hub / Go / Core) and stay online.
3. Build a **visible track record** in the community (chat, helpful publishes).
4. Get a **Level 5+ minter** to nominate you in **Q-Mintership** (you cannot nominate yourself).
5. **Minter Admins** vote on-chain (~40% yes unlocks actions; ~40% admin signatures activate the MINTER group invite).
6. Accept **JOIN_GROUP**, add a minting account in **Q-Node**, and stay online.

Open **Q-Mintership** for the live nomination board, **Q-Minter Guide** for the full written walkthrough, and **Welcome to Qortal** for first steps. Sponsorship keys are obsolete.

## Q: What are Qortal levels?

A: Levels are on-chain account levels from minting contribution, ranging from 0 to 10 — not game scores. Level 0 is the waiting room (no reward share). Level 1 is Initiation (~7,200 blocks online). Level 5 is when you may nominate others (well over a year of sync). Level 10 is Maturity (~eight years of continuous contribution). Higher levels earn a larger share of each block reward and more voting weight.

## Q: What is sponsorship or nomination?

## Q: Why is my seedphrase so important?

A: Your Qortal account is a keypair derived from the seedphrase; addresses start with "Q". The node holds no custody — there is no recovery service, support desk, or reset button. Losing the seedphrase means losing the account, names, and funds forever. Write it offline on paper (or another offline backup), never screenshot it into cloud photos, and never share it.

## Q: What is QDN?

A: QDN (Qortal Data Network) is Qortal's decentralized data layer. Every resource is identified by a registered name, a service type (e.g. WEBSITE, APP, DOCUMENT, FILE), and an optional identifier. Nodes that follow a name store and serve that name's data. Large payloads are stored off-chain and verified by an on-chain SHA-256 hash; tiny payloads under 240 bytes can live fully on-chain.

## Q: What is Q-Chat?

A: Q-Chat is Qortal's built-in messenger: public groups, private groups, and direct messages. Chat messages are unconfirmed transactions — they carry no QORT fee and quietly expire after about 24 hours, so they are not permanent chain history. Accounts typically need a small QORT balance (around 4 QORT held, not spent) as an anti-spam gate before posting freely.

## Q: What is the difference between WEBSITE and APP on QDN?

A: Both are multi-file QDN publishes. WEBSITE is for visitor-facing sites where missing paths 404. APP is for interactive apps and SPAs — missing paths fall through to index.html. APP max size is about 50 MB. Rule of thumb: client-side router and app behaviour → APP; mostly static pages with real 404s → WEBSITE.

## Q: What is a default QDN resource?

A: A resource published without an identifier is the default for that name + service — for example a user's main website is the default WEBSITE for their name. Apps can address it by omitting the identifier or using the keyword "default". Shared identifiers (like qortal_avatar) are the opposite idea: the same identifier across many names.

## Q: How do avatars work?

## Q: Are private QDN resources possible?

A: Yes. Private service variants (e.g. MAIL_PRIVATE, IMAGE_PRIVATE, DOCUMENT_PRIVATE) store data encrypted so only the intended recipient's private key can decrypt. Group-encrypted data is readable by group members. Public services are visible to anyone whose node can fetch them.
