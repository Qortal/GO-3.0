## Q: Is Qortal a cryptocurrency?

A: Qortal is more than a coin. It is an open network that people use to publish websites and apps, chat, hold an identity and trade, running on nodes its own users operate. QORT is the network's native coin: it pays network fees and rewards the minters who keep the chain going. QORT is part of Qortal, but Qortal itself is the infrastructure.

## Q: What is the difference between Qortal and QORT?

A: Qortal is the network: the blockchain, the Qortal Data Network (QDN), the Q-Apps and the people running nodes. QORT is the network's native coin, used for fees, payments and minting rewards. In short, Qortal is the blockchain and QORT is the coin.

## Q: Why is Qortal called an alternative internet?

A: Because it offers its own versions of things the usual internet gets from companies: websites and apps hosted on QDN instead of web hosts, registered names instead of domain names, plus messaging, wallets and peer-to-peer trading, all on nodes run by users. It still travels over normal internet connections today.

## Q: Does Qortal ask for ID or KYC?

A: No. Creating a Qortal account needs no ID document, and it is done on your own device. An account has no link to your legal identity; your registered name and your on-chain activity become your identity on the network. Outside services you choose to use, such as an exchange, may have their own rules, but Qortal itself does not do KYC.

## Q: What is the difference between my address and my public key?

A: Your seed phrase is the master secret that generates your account; never share it. Your public key comes from it and proves you own the account without revealing the secret, and apps use it to encrypt things for you. Your address is a shorter identifier calculated from the public key, used for payments and on-chain references. Think of them as root password, identity and account number.

## Q: Is it safe to share my Qortal address?

Your keys, which control the account, stay on your own device in an encrypted wallet file opened with your password, plus any backups you make. No company server holds your account.

## Q: How is new QORT created?

A: Only through block rewards. Each new block, roughly one a minute, creates a set amount of new QORT, which is shared out together with that block's fees, mostly among online minters. Nobody can create QORT any other way, and none existed before Genesis.

## Q: Why was QORA rebuilt as Qortal?

A: QORA was a blockchain launched in 2014 with ideas such as hosting data on the network. But it used proof of stake, which lets money buy power, and it had a bloated chain, an over-inflated coin supply and a tangled codebase; its founding team eventually abandoned it. In 2016 crowetic gathered a development group that offered the QORA community a complete rebuild: keep the good ideas, fix the flaws and remove proof of stake entirely.

## Q: How is a Qortal address made?

A: Your address is calculated from your account's public key: the key is hashed (SHA-256, then RIPEMD-160), a version byte goes in front, and a short checksum goes at the end. The result is written in Base58, giving 34 characters that start with Q. Because it comes from your key, you cannot choose or change your address; a registered name is the readable alternative.

Also asked as: how is a qortal address created · qortal address derived · where does my qortal address come from · qortal address from public key · qortal address format explained

## Q: How many characters are in a Qortal address?

A: A Qortal account address is 34 characters long and starts with Q. It uses Base58, a set of letters and digits that leaves out easily confused characters such as 0, O, I and l. If what you copied has more or fewer than 34 characters, part of the address is missing or extra.

## Q: Does a Qortal address have a checksum?

A: Yes. The end of every Qortal address is a checksum calculated from the rest, so a mistyped or partly copied address almost always shows as invalid instead of pointing at someone else. It cannot catch a valid address that belongs to the wrong person, so check the whole address before you send: on-chain payments cannot be reversed.

## Q: What is a public key on Qortal?

A: Your public key is the public half of your account's key pair, an Ed25519 key usually shown as about 44 characters. The network uses it to check that a transaction really was signed by your account, and apps use it to encrypt messages and data that only you can open. Your address is calculated from it. It is safe to share; your seed phrase and private key are not.

## Q: Why is my public key not on the blockchain yet?

A: A brand-new account is known to the network only by its address. Its public key is recorded on-chain the first time the account makes an outgoing transaction, such as a payment, a name registration or joining a group; the community wiki calls this activating the account. Until then, things that need your public key, such as others sending you encrypted messages, may not work.

## Q: Why would a Qortal transaction cost more than 0.01 QORT?

A: The 0.01 QORT fee is a unit fee: Core charges one unit for each started 1,024 bytes of transaction data. ## Q: How much new QORT is created each day?

A: There is no fixed daily number: it is the current reward per block times the number of blocks that day, about 1,300 to 1,440 blocks at 60 to 65 seconds each. For live figures, open the minting details in Hub, which show the average blocks per day and the average QORT created per day.

## Q: What is the maximum size of one Qortal block?

A: Up to 2 MB (2,097,152 bytes) per block. Big files on QDN stay off-chain, and only small publish records with hashes go into blocks, so ordinary use is far below that limit.

## Q: What is a re-org on Qortal?

A: Sometimes parts of the network briefly build on different blocks. Each node compares the competing chains and switches to the one with more chain weight, which comes mostly from how many online minters are counted in its blocks. Blocks dropped this way are orphaned, and their transactions go back to waiting for a block. It usually settles within a couple of blocks and is normal.

## Q: Why do Qortal block times vary?

A: Each block must be stamped between 30 and 90 seconds after the one before, with 60 seconds as the target. Where it lands in that window depends on how close the signer's key is to that block's ideal key and on the signer's level, so the gap changes from block to block. The community wiki puts the long-run average at around 65 seconds.

## Q: Do Qortal polls have an end date?

A: Not on-chain. A Qortal poll stores only its name, description, options and when it was created: there is no built-in closing time, and votes can be cast or changed at any time. A poll cannot be edited or deleted once created either. Any deadline is a rule of the app or group using the poll, not of the blockchain.

## Q: How do changes to Qortal's rules take effect?

A: Through Core updates. New rules are written into a Core release as feature triggers, set to switch on at a chosen block height or time, so every updated node changes behaviour at the same moment. The release reaches nodes through auto-update once about 40% of the Dev Admins approve it on-chain. Nodes left on an old version can fall out of step with the network.

## Q: Does Qortal have gas fees like Ethereum?

A: Not in that sense. Ordinary transactions only need the minimum fee: 0.01 QORT for each started 1,024 bytes, the same for everyone, and Hub and GO show the fee before you confirm. ATs, Qortal's smart contracts, pay a tiny fee for each step of code they run (0.00000001 QORT per step), taken from the AT's own balance.

## Q: What is a hash, and why does Qortal use them?

A: A hash is a short digital fingerprint of some data: change a single byte and the fingerprint changes completely. Qortal uses SHA-256 hashes widely. QDN records a hash of each publish on-chain, so anyone can check that the copy they downloaded is exactly what was published, and your address is made by hashing your public key.

## Q: Can I find out who owns a Qortal address?

A: Only what that account shows publicly. ## Q: What happens if no one is minting?

A: New blocks need online minters, so without any the chain simply pauses: nobody produces new blocks, but nothing already recorded is lost. When minters come back online, block production carries on. Qortal Core even has a recovery mode that lets minters restart a stalled network on top of older blocks.

## Q: Who decides the QORT block reward?

A: The schedule is written into Qortal Core's rules: 5 QORT per block at Genesis, 0.25 less every 259,200 blocks, down to 2 QORT. After that, the qortal.dev wiki says a vote can adjust the reward once a year by 0.25 to 1 QORT, never below 1 or above 5 QORT per block. Any change to the rules reaches nodes as a Core update approved on-chain by the Dev Admins.
