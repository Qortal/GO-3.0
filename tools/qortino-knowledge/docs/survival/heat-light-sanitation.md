# Heat, Light, and Sanitation Without the Grid

*Educational preparedness information. Carbon monoxide and fire are the two
killers in blackout winters — every combustion warning here is serious.*

## Staying warm when heating fails

Heat the person, then a room, then (maybe) the house:

- **Person:** wool or synthetic base layers (never cotton against skin when
  cold — it holds moisture and chills you), insulated hat and socks indoors,
  a hot water bottle in the sleeping bag, warm drinks and fatty food. A
  quality sleeping bag rated to your climate's extremes is a heating system
  that never needs fuel.
- **One warm room:** pick the smallest room on the sheltered side, close all
  doors, hang blankets over doorways and windows (most heat leaves through
  glass), get everyone + candles/lamp in it. Body heat alone keeps a small
  sealed room several degrees above the rest of the house.
- **Safe heat sources:** a wood stove is the gold standard. Portable
  paraffin/kerosene heaters and catalytic propane heaters rated for indoor
  use need a cracked window for oxygen and a working carbon-monoxide alarm
  with fresh batteries. NEVER heat with a barbecue, camp stove, oven left
  open (gas), or generator indoors — these kill quietly every winter.
- **Protect the house:** if freezing is prolonged, drip taps or shut off and
  drain water lines to prevent burst pipes; pour a cup of salt water or
  antifreeze rated for plumbing into toilet bowls and traps.

Recognize hypothermia early: intense shivering, clumsy hands, slurred
speech, apathy — treat with dry insulation, warm sweet drinks if fully
conscious, skin-to-skin or heat packs to the torso (not extremities first),
and gentle handling.

## Light

Priority order: LED headlamps (hands-free, efficient — a good one runs
50-150 hours on low), LED lanterns for room light, then flame (candles, oil
lamps) as ambience-plus-backup with hard rules — stable holders, away from
curtains, never asleep or unattended, one fire extinguisher and a smoke
alarm per level. Lithium primary AA/AAA cells store 10-20 years without
leaking; alkalines leak and ruin devices in storage. Rechargeables + a small
solar panel make light self-sustaining. Glow sticks are child-safe night
lights with zero fire risk.

## Sanitation: the underrated lifesaver

Disease follows failed sanitation within days. If the toilet won't flush
because the power or water is out, you still have options:

- **If sewer lines are intact** (power out, pipes fine): flush manually by
  pouring 6-8 L of any non-potable water (rain, pool, ditch) directly into
  the bowl in one confident pour.
- **If sewage may back up or water is too precious:** the twin-bucket
  system — one bucket for urine (low hazard, dilute and dispose away from
  water sources), one lined with heavy bags for feces, covered after each
  use with sawdust, ash, dry soil, or shredded paper to control odor and
  flies. Seal, store away from living areas, dispose per authorities later
  or bury 20+ cm deep, 60+ m from any water.
- **Handwashing is non-negotiable:** set up a station — jug with tap or
  bottle with pierced cap, soap, towel — beside the toilet bucket and
  kitchen. Alcohol gel (60%+) backs it up. Most "disaster diseases" are
  fecal-oral and stop at clean hands.
- Stock: toilet paper depth, heavy trash bags, soap in quantity, bleach
  (rotate yearly), moist wipes, feminine hygiene supplies, and diapers where
  relevant.

## Washing and waste

Dish hygiene without running water: three-basin method — hot soapy wash,
hot rinse, sanitizing rinse (capful of bleach per few litres), air dry.
Body: a 2-3 L "bucket bath" or pressurized garden sprayer with warmed water
keeps a person genuinely clean. Laundry: soak-agitate-wring in buckets (a
clean toilet plunger makes an effective agitator); wool clothing needs
washing far less often than cotton. Burnable trash reduces volume; food
waste attracts animals — bury or seal it.
